Reported Speech: "Had Had"

Short Overview

Many students get confused when they see had had. The first had is grammar (Past Perfect). The second had is the main verb (have).

In grammar exercises, we often use the full reported speech form:

She said she had just had her interview.

However, in everyday conversation many native speakers simplify it:

She said she just had her interview.

Remember:
Formal grammar often uses full backshifting.
Real-life English is often shorter and simpler.

Example 1

Direct speech:

"I've just had my interview."

Formal reported speech:

She said she had just had her interview.

Common spoken version:

She said she just had her interview.

Example 2

Direct speech:

"I've just had lunch."

Formal reported speech:

He said he had just had lunch.

Common spoken version:

He said he just had lunch.

Example 3

Direct speech:

"I've already had a meeting with the manager."

Formal reported speech:

She said she had already had a meeting with the manager.

Common spoken version:

She said she already had a meeting with the manager.

Example 4

Direct speech:

"I've had enough of this project."

Formal reported speech:

He said he had had enough of the project.

Common spoken version:

He said he had enough of the project.

Example 5

Direct speech:

"We've already had dinner."

Formal reported speech:

They said they had already had dinner.

Common spoken version:

They said they already had dinner.

Simple Rule

If the original sentence contains:

have had

and you backshift it into reported speech, it usually becomes:

had had

Example:

I've had my interview.
⬇
She said she had had her interview.
