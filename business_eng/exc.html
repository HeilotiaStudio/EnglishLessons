English Tenses — Auth System Practice

Past simple · past continuous · past perfect · future forms
How to use: type your answer in each blank, then press Check answers. Punctuation and capital letters are ignored, and contractions are accepted (couldn't = could not). But the tense itself must be right — fixed and had fixed are different answers.
Core idea: English signals time with the verb, not with extra words. Use past simple for a finished action at a finished time. Use past continuous for something in progress around that time. Use past perfect only when you need to show that one past action happened before another. Use will / going to for future plans, and will have + past participle for something that will be finished by a future deadline.

1. The situation (read first)

Mark is a backend developer. He could not keep working on the auth system until the external provider fixed a login bug. The fix came through on Tuesday. He then continued, but the team had already lost two days. Next sprint they are going to add biometric authentication. By the end of Q3 they will have shipped it.

3. Past continuous — action in progress

UseExampleSignal words
Action in progress at a past momentMark was working on the auth system at 10 a.m.at 10 a.m., all morning, while
Background action interrupted by a shorter oneHe was debugging when the provider called.when + past simple
Two actions happening at the same timeShe was testing while he was writing docs.while, as
Your example: "Mark couldn't keep working until the provider fixed it" is past simple — a finished state with a clear endpoint ("until… fixed"). If you say "Mark was working on the auth system when the provider fixed the bug," you are describing the action in progress at the moment of interruption. Both are correct; the choice depends on what you want to focus on.

4. Past perfect — the "before that" tense

UseExampleSignal words
An action finished before another past actionThe provider had fixed the bug before Mark resumed.before, after, already, by the time
A past result visible at a past momentThe team had lost two days by Tuesday.by Tuesday, by then
Reported speech / regretHe said he hadn't known about the bug.said, realised, wished
Rule of thumb: only reach for past perfect when the order of two past events would otherwise be unclear. If you have "before" or "after" in the sentence, past simple often works too: The provider fixed it before Mark resumed is fine. Past perfect is the safe choice when you want the "earlier" action to be unmistakable: The provider had fixed it by the time Mark resumed.

5. Future forms — the biometric auth next steps

FormUseExample

Your next-step scenario: the team is going to add biometric authentication (a plan already decided). While they are building it, they will be testing on real devices (in progress at a future time). And by the release date they will have shipped it (finished by a deadline). Three different future forms, three different jobs.
